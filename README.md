# CS201 Homework Submission Patterns

**Timing, Retries, Score Progression, and Midterm Outcomes**
STATS 401 Final Project · Zaozao Wang and Zhengxiang Liu

When homework scores cluster near full marks, how do students' submission processes differ? The main page is **a playable data film** driven by the 30 September 2026 Beijing snapshot, with two content routes: **Core** presents the five essential figures (01, 03, 11, 12, 06) and **Showcase** presents all twelve. Each of the 2,807 in-window submissions is drawn as an aggregate unit (a particle) that forms the opening “100”, lands in its four-hour window on a spiral clock and re-forms between figures; particles are assigned from published counts, not tracked identities. The analysis companion keeps all twelve static figures, tables and downloads, and links each one back to its reading view in the film.

Two previously pending analyses are now implemented from ordered event data: eligible retries setting a new personal best, and new-best retries within five equal-duration phases of each homework window. Rates include their numerators, denominators and distinct contributors. The 1,222 eligible retries include 468 new bests (38.3%); repeated events are not independent students. Four deadline-phase cells and two problem cells are masked for disclosure control. See [event methods](docs/event-methods.md).

## The data film

The film plays like a video and pauses like a dashboard. Two independent choices control it:

**Content** (the primary switch in the header and on the title screen):

- **Core · 5 essential figures** — 01 timing, reach and repeat intensity → 03 remaining problem gaps after three attempts → 11 eligible retries that set a new best → 12 retry progress within the homework window → 06 pre-exam behavior and midterm associations. A brief opening shows what the 30 September snapshot added (HW4: 706 submissions, eight problem slots) and a sample-switch bridge introduces the exam's separate cohort (26 supplied grades, pre-exam HW1–HW3, HW4 excluded) before Figure 06. About 3½ minutes.
- **Showcase · All 12 figures** — sample context → 01 → 02 → 03 → 04 → 11 → 12 → sample switch → 05 → 06 → 07 → 08 → 09 → 10 → conclusion. Course Pulse coverage (Figure 08) comes before the platform results. About 6 minutes.

**Playback** (secondary, in the transport bar): **Presenter** (default) plays each beat and holds on the finished chart until you continue; **Auto** plays straight through; **Loop** is a 60-second highlight reel per route (`assets/app/reel.js`), cut to Glass Animals' "The Other Side of Paradise" at a fixed 128.000 BPM and following its bars 104–135. The Core reel only cuts from Core chapters. A highlight reel is an edit, not proof that every figure was shown. The song is not part of the site: load your own copy with **♪** and the reel locks to it; the file never leaves the computer.

Switching content always pauses and never starts playback. It keeps the current figure when both routes contain it (the same frame), and otherwise lands on the closest preceding Core figure in Showcase order with a visible notice. Every figure chapter shows its sample caption (for example `Eligible retry events · 468 / 1,222 set a new best`) and links directly to its analysis section, methods and PNG/SVG/PDF downloads. The homework lens is hidden for exam and platform figures, which use a different, pre-exam sample.

Every frame is a pure function of the timeline position and the viewer's interaction state, so you can pause, scrub or deep-link to any moment; the approach follows [mexicat/pdoom-video](https://github.com/mexicat/pdoom-video). Charts are drawn with D3 v7 (vendored in `assets/vendor/`), SVG and Canvas 2D. With *reduced motion* the film uses fades instead of zooms and slams, freezes the starfield and never autoplays.

Scrolling or swiping scrubs the timeline.

Files: `index.html` (shell), `assets/app/figures.json` (the shared figure-to-route and evidence mapping; also read by `scripts/render_site.py`), `assets/app/routes.js` (both route scripts, captions, reading stops, deep links and switching rules), `core.js` (math, palette, shared state, layout), `particles.js`, `reel.js` (the two Loop edits), `player.js` (one playback owner: timeline, transport, captions, transitions, soundtrack clock) and `assets/app/scenes/*.js` (one module per scene; several figures can share a scene). After changing `data/*.json` or `figures.json`, run `uv run python scripts/build_app_data.py` to regenerate `assets/app/data.js` and `assets/app/figures.js`. After editing any film asset, bump the `?v=` tag on the asset links in `index.html` so browsers and GitHub Pages skip their cached copies. Homework hues were validated for all-pairs colour-vision separation on the dark background. Fonts load from Google Fonts, with system-font fallbacks.

## Scope and interpretation

The new snapshot was taken on 29 September at 16:27:16 UTC, or 30 September at 00:27:16 Beijing time. After locally applying the previously confirmed test-account exclusion, it contains 42 roster members and 3,070 course submissions. Four homework windows retain 2,807 submissions from 39 members across 31 problem slots; 93 homework events fall outside the configured windows. The original private export is unchanged. Public temporal cells are four hours wide and positive cells with fewer than five contributors are masked. The source export, student pseudonyms, individual scores and linkage key are private.

Submission counts are not measures of effort; submission gaps are not study duration. Best scores cannot decrease by definition, and score progression does not establish causal learning improvement. The public timing figure includes only the 37 positive cells meeting the five-contributor threshold; 979 submissions remain in masked cells. Homework windows use configured deadlines and do not incorporate individual extensions. See the methods for denominators and normalization.

## Reading the new attempt-score figure

For each homework, attempt k means the kth submission by the same student to the same problem within its configured window. We average that event's normalized score across pairs that actually reach k, with equal weight per event. Students retrying multiple problems can contribute multiple events. The N row counts events; S counts unique students. There is **no carry-forward and no running-best substitution**. Later points represent a smaller, different set of student–problem pairs; a falling mean does not show that a fixed group got worse. Points with fewer than five students are withheld.
