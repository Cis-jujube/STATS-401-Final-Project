# CS201 Homework Submission Patterns

**Timing, Retries, Score Progression, and Midterm Outcomes**
STATS 401 Final Project · Zaozao Wang and Zhengxiang Liu

When homework scores cluster near full marks, how do students' submission processes differ? The main page is now **a playable data film**: eight D3 scenes on one timeline, driven by the 30 September 2026 Beijing snapshot. Every one of the 2,807 in-window submissions is a particle that travels between scenes. It forms the opening “100”, stacks by homework, and lands in its real four-hour window on a spiral clock. The analysis companion keeps all twelve static figures, tables and downloads.

Two previously pending analyses are now implemented from ordered event data: eligible retries setting a new personal best, and new-best retries within five equal-duration phases of each homework window. Rates include their numerators, denominators and distinct contributors. The 1,222 eligible retries include 468 new bests (38.3%); repeated events are not independent students. Four deadline-phase cells and two problem cells are masked for disclosure control. See [event methods](docs/event-methods.md).

## The data film

The film plays like a video and pauses like a dashboard. It has **two modes**, switched in the transport bar or on the title screen:

- **Presenter** (default) is the full 4½-minute story. Each beat plays its animation, then holds on the finished chart while you talk; the background keeps drifting. Press → / Space / Enter or a presentation clicker (PageDown) to continue, and ← / PageUp to go back a chart. **Auto** plays straight through without holds.
- **Loop** is a 60-second highlight reel defined in `assets/app/reel.js`, cut to Glass Animals' "The Other Side of Paradise". The track runs at a fixed 128.000 BPM, measured from the audio with its first beat at 0.260 s, and the reel follows its bars 104–135 (3:16.20–4:16.20) phrase by phrase: long shots through the build, the spiral winding up over the steady drive, one cut per bar as it rises, and a punch-in plus one-beat flurries at the peak. The last frame dissolves into the first, so it repeats seamlessly, which makes it suitable for an unattended showcase screen (`index.html?loop&autoplay`). The song is not part of the site. Load your own copy with **♪** and the reel recognises it by file name, starts it at bar 104 and loops those 32 bars together with the picture. Any other track works too: tap the tempo and nudge *Start at* onto a downbeat. The file never leaves the computer.

 Every frame is a pure function of the timeline position and the viewer's interaction state. You can pause, scrub or deep-link to any moment and get the same picture; the approach follows [mexicat/pdoom-video](https://github.com/mexicat/pdoom-video). Charts are drawn with D3 v7 (vendored in `assets/vendor/`), SVG and Canvas 2D.

| # | Scene | Idiom | What the viewer can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | Opening | Particle numeral (2,807 submissions) | Disturb the particles with the pointer |
| 00 | Four homeworks | Unit chart, one dot per submission | Hover a column for its window and attempt spread; click to set the lens |
| 01 | Time | **Spiral calendar**, the complex non-linear temporal view: dot density ⇄ heat map | Calendar ⇄ Clock morph; Dots ⇄ Heat; measure: submissions, students, or per student; hovering a window draws a polar crosshair (that day's ring + that time of day across all days) |
| 02 | Problems | Multi-stage dot plot (“roads”) for 31 problems | Draggable stage slider that scrubs every problem continuously from first try to final best; four sort orders; click a problem to select it across scenes |
| 03 | Attempts | Particle histogram of all 2,807 submissions by try number; log-scale box plots; k-th try line chart with a linked cohort panel | Tries ⇄ Spread ⇄ Cohort; all submissions vs until first full score; crosshair details |
| 04 | Retries | Particles split into first tries / retries / after full marks, then the 1,222 retries become a waffle that ignites; small-multiple phase bars; problem bubble scatter | Switch views; hover for counts; the problem selected in 02 stays ringed |
| 05 | Midterm | Unit dots; forest plot with bootstrap CIs and leave-one-out bands; box plots | Robustness check (all / leave one out / full coverage / no discrepancies); group by submissions or Course Pulse |
| — | Finale | Rewind montage (hard cuts back through the film under a VHS overlay); takeaway cards; the numeral re-forms | Jump back to the chapter behind each takeaway |

Cinematic layer: each chapter opens with an outlined numeral intertitle that settles into a watermark behind the caption, letterbox bars and a flash at every cut, a zoom-through exit around the outgoing scene's focal point, and decoding terminal-style labels. Playback feedback: a centre play/pause pop, a timecode that rides above the playhead while scrubbing, click ripples, a cursor light and a parallax starfield.

Coordinated interactions: the **homework lens** in the header highlights one homework in every chart. The **selected problem** is shared between chapters 02 and 04. Touching any chart control pauses the film; each beat change restores the director's view.

Controls: <kbd>Space</kbd> play/pause · <kbd>→</kbd>/<kbd>PageDown</kbd>/<kbd>Enter</kbd> continue · <kbd>←</kbd>/<kbd>PageUp</kbd> back · <kbd>Shift</kbd>+arrows ±5 s · <kbd>[</kbd>/<kbd>]</kbd> scenes · <kbd>,</kbd>/<kbd>.</kbd> frame step · <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>8</kbd> jump · <kbd>P</kbd> Presenter · <kbd>L</kbd> Loop · <kbd>A</kbd> Auto · <kbd>H</kbd> hide UI · <kbd>F</kbd> fullscreen. Scrolling or swiping scrubs the timeline. The film opens on a poster and waits for Play (`?autoplay` starts immediately). Deep links open paused on that frame: `index.html#time`, `#problems`, `#retries`, `#exam`, or `?t=95` for a time in seconds.

Files: `index.html` (shell), `assets/app/core.js` (math, palette, shared state), `particles.js` (submission particles), `reel.js` (the Loop edit list), `player.js` (both timelines, transport, captions, transitions, soundtrack clock) and `assets/app/scenes/*.js` (one module per scene). After changing anything in `data/*.json`, run `python3 scripts/build_app_data.py` to regenerate `assets/app/data.js`. After editing any film asset, bump the `?v=` tag on the asset links in `index.html`, so browsers and GitHub Pages skip their cached copies. Homework hues were validated for all-pairs colour-vision separation on the dark background. Fonts load from Google Fonts, with system-font fallbacks.

## Reproduce from public aggregates

Python 3.13 and Matplotlib 3.11.2 are captured by `.python-version`, `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock`. The user approved the project-local dependency. No global installation is needed.

```sh
uv sync --locked
uv run python scripts/render_figures.py
uv run python scripts/render_figures.py --web
uv run python scripts/render_events.py
uv run python scripts/render_events.py --web
uv run python scripts/render_midterm.py
uv run python scripts/render_platform.py
uv run python scripts/build_page.py
uv run python scripts/build_app_data.py
uv run python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

The page builder assembles the same aggregate evidence with `scripts/render_site.py` and the HTML fragments in `scripts/templates/`. `styles.css` and `assets/site.js` provide the native CSS/JavaScript presentation. The `--web` homework export writes four desktop and four mobile SVG compositions to `assets/story/`; the midterm and platform renderers export their story assets separately. Publication PNG/PDF/SVG files remain in `assets/figures/`. No frontend install or development server is required.

The original renderer exports eight compositions (four desktop + four mobile); `render_events.py` adds four (two desktop + two mobile), each as a 300 DPI PNG and vector SVG/PDF. It follows [figures4papers / scientific-figure-making](https://github.com/ChenLiu-1996/figures4papers/tree/3c181f85e82c6f24948fcaaf3be6696102b41d8d/scientific-figure-making): shared sans-serif typography, minimal spines, consistent colors, frameless legends and vector text. See [style provenance](docs/figure-style.md). The previous optional Sharp rasterizer is retained for historical compatibility; it is not required for the current pipeline.

The output remains beside that private JSON. Do not put raw data or private outputs into this repository.

## Scope and interpretation

The new snapshot was taken on 29 September at 16:27:16 UTC, or 30 September at 00:27:16 Beijing time. After locally applying the previously confirmed test-account exclusion, it contains 42 roster members and 3,070 course submissions. Four homework windows retain 2,807 submissions from 39 members across 31 problem slots; 93 homework events fall outside the configured windows. The original private export is unchanged. Public temporal cells are four hours wide and positive cells with fewer than five contributors are masked. The source export, student pseudonyms, individual scores and linkage key are private.

Submission counts are not measures of effort; submission gaps are not study duration. Best scores cannot decrease by definition, and score progression does not establish causal learning improvement. The public timing figure includes only the 37 positive cells meeting the five-contributor threshold; 979 submissions remain in masked cells. Homework windows use configured deadlines and do not incorporate individual extensions. See the methods for denominators and normalization.

## Reading the new attempt-score figure

For each homework, attempt k means the kth submission by the same student to the same problem within its configured window. We average that event's normalized score across pairs that actually reach k, with equal weight per event. Students retrying multiple problems can contribute multiple events. The N row counts events; S counts unique students. There is **no carry-forward and no running-best substitution**. Later points represent a smaller, different set of student–problem pairs; a falling mean does not show that a fixed group got worse. Points with fewer than five students are withheld.
